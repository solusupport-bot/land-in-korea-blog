## The detail that catches almost everyone off guard

Most first-timers plan their Seoul itinerary around Gyeongbokgung without checking one thing: the palace is closed every Tuesday. If a Tuesday falls on a public holiday, it stays open and the closure shifts to the next non-holiday day instead. It's a small detail, but it derails more first-day plans than anything else on this list.

## It's not just a photo backdrop

Built in 1395, Gyeongbokgung is Seoul's largest and most significant palace, and the grounds also hold the National Folk Museum of Korea, included in your visit at no extra cost. Budget more time than a quick walk-through — between the throne hall, the pavilion over the pond, and the museum, a rushed 20-minute stop misses most of it.

## The free ceremony most people miss

The Royal Guard Changing Ceremony runs at the Heungnyemun Gate at set times, not continuously — currently around 10:00 and 14:00, with a shorter guard dispatch ceremony at 11:00 and 13:00. Showing up between those windows means missing it entirely, so check the schedule before you plan your visit around it. It's held daily except palace closure days, and it's completely free to watch.

## The hanbok trick that actually works

Wearing a complete hanbok that meets the palace's official guidelines gets you free admission — one of the few tourist perks in Seoul that's both genuinely useful and easy to use, since hanbok rental shops cluster right outside the palace gates. It also makes for noticeably better photos than street clothes against the traditional architecture. Just note that a rental shop's promise isn't the same as the palace's actual guidelines, so a genuine hanbok (not just any traditional-looking outfit) is what qualifies.

## Pair it with Bukchon

Bukchon Hanok Village sits within walking distance, making it a natural add-on after the palace rather than a separate trip. Most visitors do both in one afternoon.

## The short version

- Closed Tuesdays (unless it's a holiday)
- Guard ceremony runs on a schedule (currently ~10:00 and 14:00) — not continuous
- A full hanbok gets you in free
- Budget more than 20 minutes — it's Seoul's largest palace with a museum on-site
- Bukchon Hanok Village is a short walk away
