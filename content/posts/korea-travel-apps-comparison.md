Google Maps works fine for getting to Korea. It's noticeably weaker once you're actually navigating inside it — Google's local transit and walking-direction data in Korea has real gaps that most first-timers don't discover until they're already lost.

## Naver Map vs. KakaoMap: pick either, both beat Google here

Both apps show real-time subway and bus arrival times, platform-level transfer directions inside stations, and walking routes that actually match Korean street data. Most visitors don't discover the platform-level transfer directions until well into their trip — it's the single most useful feature neither Google Maps nor most people's home country app has an equivalent for. Naver Map also integrates local restaurant review data that tends to be more current than international review apps.

## Papago, for everything language-related

Papago handles Korean translation more naturally than general-purpose translation apps for this specific context, including camera translation for menus and street signs — genuinely useful when a menu has no English at all.

## Kakao T, for taxis

Kakao T is the go-to app for calling a taxi reliably in Korea, especially late at night or in areas where flagging one down from the street is difficult. Fares are estimated upfront in the app before you confirm.

## The one setup step people skip

Download Naver Map, Papago, and Kakao T *before* you land — some functions work more reliably with a local IP or an active Korean SIM/eSIM, and fumbling with app store downloads on spotty airport Wi-Fi is a bad first impression of a trip.
