Almost every Korea travel guide tells you "get an eSIM" and moves on. That's fine advice for a solo 5-day trip — and the wrong advice for a lot of other trips. Here's what actually changes the answer.

## The three options, honestly

- **eSIM**: buy online, activate by QR code on arrival, no physical swap. Best when your phone supports it and you're traveling alone or want your own separate data plan per person.
- **Physical SIM**: pick up at an airport counter, swaps out your existing SIM. Works on phones without eSIM support.
- **Pocket Wi-Fi (Wi-Fi egg)**: one device, rented for the trip, an entire group connects to it. You return the device before you fly out.

![pocket wifi egg rental counter](https://images.pexels.com/photos/18936020/pexels-photo-18936020.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)


## Where each one wins

| Trip shape | Best option | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Solo traveler, newer phone | eSIM | No counter line, no device to return, keep your home number reachable on a second slot |
| Couple or family sharing a data budget | Pocket Wi-Fi | One rental instead of 2-4 separate SIMs — usually the cheaper total once you add it up per person |
| Older phone without eSIM | Physical SIM | eSIM isn't an option for you regardless of price |
| Trip under 4 days | Whichever is fastest to get, don't overthink it | The convenience difference matters more than the price difference at this length |
| Trip over 10 days | eSIM or physical SIM, skip pocket Wi-Fi | Carrying and charging a separate device for a long trip gets old; per-person data plans age better |

## The actual mistake

Buying data for the exact number of calendar days you're in Korea, arrival day to departure day. Most plans are sold in fixed blocks (5-day, 8-day) — if your trip is 6 days, you're paying for 8 either way, so it's worth checking whether a longer block is only marginally more expensive than the shorter one before you buy.
