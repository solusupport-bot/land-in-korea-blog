## The short version

Insadong looks like a tourist strip until you realize it's Seoul's actual center for traditional Korean art, antiques, and hanji (traditional paper) — not a themed recreation. The main street bans cars every Sunday, turning it into a full pedestrian zone with street performers and craft stalls. Ssamziegil, a spiral-shaped shopping complex just off the main road, packs small design studios and craft shops into one building worth a dedicated stop. Traditional tea houses (전통찻집) tucked into side alleys serve tea the old way — order one and sit for a while rather than treating it like a quick coffee stop.

Most visitors walk straight down Insadong's main street and miss the side alleys where the actual antique dealers and gallery spaces sit. Jogyesa Temple, one of Korean Buddhism's most important temples, is a short walk from Insadong's north end and free to enter. Calligraphy supplies, hanbok rental shops, and traditional craft workshops cluster here more than almost anywhere else in Seoul. Weekday mornings are dramatically quieter than weekend afternoons, when the pedestrian street fills with both tourists and local visitors.
