A jjimjilbang (찜질방) is a Korean public bathhouse complex. It's part spa, part sauna, part hostel. You pay a small entry fee (10,000-15,000 won, or $7-11 USD), remove your shoes, and access bathrooms, saunas, pools, resting areas, and sometimes sleeping rooms.

It's uniquely Korean and a legitimate cultural experience. But there are strict etiquette rules. Breaking them will get you looks, complaints, or removal. This guide explains how to use a jjimjilbang without mistakes.

## What Is a Jjimjilbang?

It's not just a bathhouse. It's a multipurpose facility:
- Bathing area (nude, separated by gender)
- Saunas (dry saunas, wet saunas, special rooms)
- Pools (sometimes separate men/women, sometimes co-ed)
- Sleeping area (you can sleep here overnight)
- Snack bar/restaurant
- Resting room with heated floor (everyone uses together)
- Sometimes massages or other services

**Entry fee:** 10,000-15,000 won. Extra costs for special services (massage, food).

**Hours:** Usually 24 hours or close 1-5 AM.

**Why Koreans use them:** Cultural bathing tradition, affordable, social gathering, place to sleep cheaply.

## Jjimjilbang Etiquette: The Rules

### The Bathing Area (Nude, Gender-Separated)

**Rule 1: You are nude. This is normal.**
The bathing area is completely nude. No swimsuits. No towels. This is standard practice, and Koreans see nudity in this context as non-sexual. Comfort yourself with the fact that everyone else is nude too.

**Rule 2: Wash before entering any pool or saunas.**
This is mandatory, not optional. There are shower stations before each pool/sauna.
- Soap entire body
- Rinse completely
- This ensures the pools stay clean for everyone
Failure to wash is considered very disrespectful.

**Rule 3: No soap in the pools.**
Wash beforehand only. Never soap your body in the pool itself.

**Rule 4: Discreet behavior in bathing area.**
Don't stare at other people. Don't touch others. Keep to yourself. This is a respectful, quiet environment despite nudity.

**Rule 5: Keep your own shower/bathing area clean.**
Rinse it after you use it. Wipe your tub. Leave no mess.

**Rule 6: Don't take photos.**
Absolutely no photography in bathing areas. Phones should not be brought in at all.

**Rule 7: Hair and body standards.**
There are no rules about body hair or appearance. All bodies are welcome. This is egalitarian.

### The Sauna Areas (Co-Ed, Clothed)

**Rule 1: Wear a provided small towel/cloth.**
When exiting the nude bathing area, wrap yourself in the small towel provided. This is standard.

**Rule 2: Different saunas, different rules.**
- Dry saunas: Wear the towel or sit on it
- Wet saunas (steam): Minimal clothing acceptable, usually just towel
- Ice saunas: Same as dry saunas

**Rule 3: Don't sit directly on saunas.**
Always sit on the towel provided. This is hygiene protocol.

**Rule 4: Respect quiet times.**
Saunas are for relaxation. Keep noise to a minimum.

### Sleeping Area (Communal Floor)

**Rule 1: Wear clothes.**
You must wear clothes in the sleeping area. Wear light sleepwear (joggers, t-shirt) or clothes provided by the jjimjilbang.

**Rule 2: Use provided materials.**
They provide mats, pillows, blankets. Use these. Bring your own if you want, but provided is standard.

**Rule 3: No snoring disturbance.**
If your snoring disturbs others, staff might ask you to leave or change location. This is rare, but extremely loud snoring can get you in trouble.

**Rule 4: Shoes off.**
When entering the sleeping area, remove your shoes. Use the shoe locker at entrance.

### General Facility Rules

**Rule 1: Respect the foot baths.**
Many areas have foot baths at entrances. Rinse your feet before entering. It's expected.

**Rule 2: No running or loud noise.**
This is a relaxation facility. Move calmly. Speak quietly.

**Rule 3: Don't hog facilities.**
If there's a line for saunas or pools, keep your time reasonable (20-30 minutes). Others are waiting.

**Rule 4: Clean up after yourself.**
Water spills, soap residue, hair—clean what you can. Staff will clean, but respect the space.

**Rule 5: Phones are okay in common areas only.**
Bathing areas and saunas: no phones. Sleeping and eating areas: phones okay but respect quiet.

## First-Timer Step-by-Step Guide

1. **Enter jjimjilbang**, remove shoes, place in shoe locker
2. **Pay entry fee** (10,000-15,000 won) at counter
3. **Receive key/wristband** with locker number
4. **Go to bathing area**
5. **Find your gender-separated section** (look for signs: 남자 men, 여자 women)
6. **Remove all clothing** and place in locker
7. **Rinse entirely** in shower station (soap entire body)
8. **Enter pool or warm bath** (start with warm water to acclimate)
9. **Try different temperatures** (hot pool, cool pool, ice bath)
10. **Visit dry saunas** (bring towel, sit on towel)
11. **Exit bathing area**, dry off, wrap in small towel
12. **Visit sauna areas** if interested (co-ed, wear towel)
13. **Visit sleeping area** if staying overnight (wear clothes)
14. **Use facilities** as desired
15. **Exit**, pay any additional costs, retrieve shoes

## First-Timer Tips

**Tip 1: Bring your own flip-flops or shower shoes.**
Most jjimjilbangs provide them, but bringing your own is more comfortable.

**Tip 2: Start with moderate temperatures.**
Don't jump into the hottest pool immediately. Build up tolerance.

**Tip 3: Hydrate.**
Saunas dehydrate. Drink water (available at snack bar) throughout your visit.

**Tip 4: Don't spend entire time in saunas.**
Alternate between saunas and cooling pools. The temperature contrast is healthy.

**Tip 5: Go off-peak if you're nervous about nudity.**
Visit Tuesday-Thursday afternoon (fewest crowds). Peak times: weekends, 7-10 PM.

**Tip 6: Don't overthink it.**
Korean people see jjimjilbang bathing as normal, non-sexual, respectful activity. Everyone is in the same situation. Discomfort fades quickly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is jjimjilbang appropriate for solo travelers?

Yes, absolutely. Solo visitors are common. You'll be in gender-separated bathing areas, so no mixed gender bathing. Very safe and normal.

### Will I be judged for my body in the bathing area?

No. Korean jjimjilbang culture is accepting of all bodies. Nudity is non-sexual here. Everyone respects privacy and keeps to themselves. You will not be scrutinized.

### What if I'm uncomfortable being completely nude?

You can skip the bathing area and use only saunas and pools (which allow towels). Or, try it once—discomfort usually disappears after 5 minutes. Many Western visitors report this as their first jjimjilbang visit and never want to go back to regular showers after.

### Is jjimjilbang sanitary?

Yes. Pools are chlorinated and tested. Saunas are cleaned frequently. The mandatory pre-wash ensures pool cleanliness. Millions of Koreans use jjimjilbangs safely. It's sanitary.
