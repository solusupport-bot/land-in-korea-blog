## What matters here

Cherry blossoms open first in the far south — Jeju, Busan, Changwon — around late March, then sweep northward, usually reaching Seoul in early April. Autumn colors run in reverse: they start in the northern mountains like Seoraksan in early-to-mid October, hit Seoul roughly late October into early November, and finish in the southern provinces afterward. Weather companies and the Korea Meteorological Administration publish bloom and foliage forecast maps each year, and those are worth checking a couple of weeks out rather than booking blind months ahead.

Blossoms only look their best for about a week, and one heavy rain or windy night can strip them, so flexibility beats a fixed date. Major festivals — Jinhae's Gunhangje, Yeouido's riverside cherry blossom road, Gyeongju, Jeonju — concentrate enormous crowds onto one or two weekends, with road closures, shuttle-only access, and trains and intercity buses selling out well in advance. Weekday mornings are dramatically quieter at the same spots, and ordinary neighborhood streams, university campuses, and apartment-complex streets bloom just as hard with almost nobody there.
