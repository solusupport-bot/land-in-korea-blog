## One of the passes you'll read about doesn't exist anymore

If you've been researching this for a while, you've probably seen Go City's Seoul pass mentioned alongside the Discover Seoul Pass. Skip it — Go City shut down its Seoul product in August 2024, though plenty of older blog posts and third-party booking listings still list it as if it's live. If an article you're reading brings up Go City for Seoul, treat that as a sign the rest of its pricing is outdated too. The two passes actually worth comparing today are the official Discover Seoul Pass and Klook's Seoul pass, plus a simpler option if you only care about palaces.

## What's actually available now

The Discover Seoul Pass, run through Seoul's tourism authority, comes in two formats: a "Pick 3" version where you choose a set number of attractions, and a time-based version (activated on first use, running 72 or 120 hours) with unlimited entry to included sites in that window. Klook's Seoul pass works differently — you pick a number of attractions from a curated list and use them at your own pace within a set number of days, no clock running once you start. Both bundle in well-known paid sights like [N Seoul Tower](https://landinkorea.com/posts/korea-n-seoul-tower-namsan.html), though the exact list on either changes over time.

## The math that actually decides if it's worth it

Add up the individual entry prices for everything you'd actually visit, then compare that total against the pass price — most people skip this step and assume bundling is automatically cheaper. It isn't, for a light schedule. Per-attraction cost drops the more attractions you select, since the pass price rises more slowly than the number of paid sites it unlocks: a 2-attraction pick tends to save little over buying separately, while a 4-6 attraction pick can genuinely undercut individual admission — but only if you were actually going to visit all of them. Padding your itinerary with a site you don't care about just to "use" the pass defeats the purpose.

## Fixed-duration vs. credit-based: which fits your trip

A fixed-duration pass (the Discover Seoul Pass's 72/120-hour option) starts a countdown the moment you first scan it, so every included attraction has to fit inside that window — good for a dense, back-to-back sightseeing run, bad if you want rest days in between. A credit-based pass (Klook's version, and the Discover Seoul Pass's "Pick 3" tier) instead gives you a set number of visits to use over a longer stretch, commonly around a month, with no clock running between them. If your trip mixes sightseeing with slower days or day trips outside the city, the credit-based structure wastes less value than one that's silently counting down while you're not using it.

## If you're only here for palaces, skip the tourist pass

None of the general attraction passes are the cheapest way to see multiple palaces. Seoul separately sells an integrated ticket covering all four major palaces — [Gyeongbokgung](https://landinkorea.com/posts/korea-gyeongbokgung-palace.html), Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung, and Deoksugung — plus Jongmyo Shrine, sold at any palace ticket window for a fraction of a general attraction pass's cost. It doesn't include Changdeokgung's Secret Garden, which needs its own reservation-limited ticket, and it covers one visit per site rather than repeat entry. If palaces are your main goal and you're not also chasing N Seoul Tower or Lotte World, this integrated ticket beats any general pass on price.

## The extras bundled in, and why you should still double-check

Beyond attraction entry, the Discover Seoul Pass has historically bundled in things that aren't "attractions" at all: a stored-value transit card, a tourist eSIM redemption, a hanbok rental credit, and a one-way airport rail ride. If you're already planning your own [T-money card](https://landinkorea.com/posts/korea-t-money-transit-card.html) or [eSIM](https://landinkorea.com/posts/korea-esim-mobile-data.html), don't count the pass's version as pure savings — you'd be paying for one anyway. More importantly, every pass's price and included-attraction list changes more often than general guides get updated. Confirm current terms on the pass's own official page before buying, not from this article or any summary.

## When a pass actually pays off

Passes work best on days packed with paid attractions back-to-back — palace, tower, museum, repeat — and worst on days mixing in free sightseeing that needs no ticket: wandering [Bukchon Hanok Village](https://landinkorea.com/posts/korea-bukchon-hanok-village.html), browsing [Myeongdong](https://landinkorea.com/posts/korea-myeongdong-shopping-district.html) or [Namdaemun Market](https://landinkorea.com/posts/korea-namdaemun-market.html). If your itinerary is naturally half free and half paid, run the math above before assuming a pass saves money — for a lot of realistic Seoul itineraries, it doesn't.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Discover Seoul Pass still worth buying in 2026?

It depends on your itinerary. Add up individual admission for everything you'd actually visit and compare it to the pass price — it tends to pay off on days packed with several paid attractions back-to-back, and lose value on lighter, free-sightseeing-heavy days.

### Can I still buy a Go City pass for Seoul?

No. Go City closed its Seoul product in August 2024. If you see it listed on a comparison site or booking platform, that source is outdated — the Discover Seoul Pass and Klook's Seoul pass are the current options.

### What's the difference between the Discover Seoul Pass and the Klook Seoul pass?

The Discover Seoul Pass offers both a fixed-duration option (72 or 120 hours, counting down from first use) and a "Pick 3" credit option. Klook's pass is credit-based only — you pick a number of attractions and use them at your own pace within a set window, without a running clock.

### Should I buy a general attraction pass just to see palaces?

No — Seoul sells a separate integrated ticket covering the four major palaces (including Gyeongbokgung) plus Jongmyo Shrine, sold at palace ticket windows, for far less than a general pass. It's the better option if palaces are your main focus.

### Does the Discover Seoul Pass include transit or an eSIM?

Historically it's bundled in extras like a transit card, an eSIM redemption, and a hanbok rental credit alongside attraction entry, but terms change, so confirm what's currently included on the official page first.

### How many attractions do I need to visit to make a pass worth it?

There's no fixed number since prices change, but the pattern holds: cost per attraction drops as you add more to your pick, so a 2-attraction bundle rarely beats buying individually while a 4-6 attraction bundle often does — provided you were genuinely planning to visit all of them.
