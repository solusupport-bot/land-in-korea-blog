Check the price before agreeing to a service, keep the receipt, and know where to ask for help. Those steps are more useful than trying to judge a situation from a stranger's friendliness or a neighborhood's reputation.

## Before paying

For a meal or a bar visit, ask to see the menu and confirm whether a price is per person or per dish. If a charge is unclear, ask before ordering. For a tour, accommodation or other booking, save the listing, cancellation terms and confirmation message.

For a taxi ride, keep the trip record or receipt. If you later question a fare, the time, route and vehicle details will be more useful than a rough estimate from memory. A booking app does not, by itself, establish that every charge is correct.

These are practical precautions, not evidence that a particular business or district is unsafe.

## If you need to raise a complaint

Write down the business name, location, date and what happened. Keep receipts, booking messages and screenshots that explain the issue. Describe the specific charge or service you dispute, and say what response you are requesting. Avoid posting accusations that you cannot support.

The Korea Tourism Organization's Tourist Complaint Center accepts tourism-related complaints involving businesses, facilities and transport, including wrongful conduct and discrimination. Its remit does not cover every dispute: individual-to-individual disputes and matters already before a court are among its exclusions.

Read the [Tourist Complaint Center's scope and submission process](https://www.touristcomplaint.or.kr/en/intro) before submitting. Its process includes email verification and review or referral to the relevant organization; a submission does not guarantee a refund.

## Help when language is the barrier

For tourism questions or help finding the appropriate service, contact the **1330 Travel Helpline** within Korea, or **+82-2-1330** from overseas. The official complaint-center page lists Korean, English, Japanese, Chinese, Russian, Vietnamese, Thai and Indonesian support.

A tourism complaint form is not an emergency response channel. If you are in immediate danger, seek emergency assistance rather than waiting for an online complaint to be reviewed.

## Make a useful record

A short, organized account is easier to explain across languages than a long account written while upset. Before contacting the business or a complaint service, collect the details you already have:

| Detail | What to keep |
|---|---|
| Business or vehicle | Business name and address, or the vehicle details shown on the receipt |
| Time | Date and approximate time of the visit or ride |
| Agreement | Menu, booking confirmation or message showing the service and quoted price |
| Payment | Itemized receipt and relevant payment record |
| Requested response | A clear explanation, a corrected charge or another specific outcome |

Keep originals of your records. If you share a screenshot publicly, cover passport details, payment-card numbers, booking codes and other private information first. Send sensitive records only through the official reporting channel when they are needed. Do not photograph people or documents simply to build a case if doing so would put you in an unsafe situation.

## Questions that help resolve a price disagreement

Start with the item you do not understand. Ask for an itemized bill, point to the charge, and compare it with the price you agreed to. A useful question is: “What is this charge for?” It gives the other person something concrete to explain.

For a booking, check whether you are looking at the total price, a deposit or a separate service charge. For a restaurant, check the quantity and whether the listed price was per serving. These checks help describe a disagreement; they do not establish that an unexpected charge is lawful or unlawful.

If the explanation still does not resolve the issue, keep the records and ask the official complaint service whether the matter falls within its scope. Avoid paying a stranger who offers to recover the money for you.
