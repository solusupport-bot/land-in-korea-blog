Korea is a technologically advanced country with apps for everything. But some of those apps are Korean-only, and some Western apps don't function reliably here. This creates a specific problem: you land with your familiar apps and immediately discover half of them don't work as expected.

Getting the right apps before arrival is worth 30 minutes of time. This guide lists the essential apps that actually work in Korea and matter for travelers.

## Navigation Apps (Critical)

### Naver Map (Essential, #1 Priority)

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** Real-time navigation for subways, buses, walking, and taxis in Korea

Naver Map is Korea's default navigation app. It shows every subway line, every bus route, real-time transit schedules, walking directions, and taxi fares. It works offline with pre-downloaded maps.

**Why Google Maps doesn't work:**
Google Maps shows Korea's streets, but subway and bus information is incomplete or inaccurate. Relying on Google Maps for transit in Korea will lead you to wrong stations or non-existent bus stops.

**How to use:**
1. Download before arrival
2. Open and allow location access
3. Enter destination (can be in Korean or English for major places)
4. Select transit type (subway/bus/walk/taxi)
5. Follow directions

**Offline usage:**
Download offline maps for Seoul, Busan, or other regions before traveling. You can navigate without internet, though real-time updates won't refresh.

### Kakao Map (Alternative, #2 Priority)

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** Similar to Naver Map; Korea's second-largest navigation app

Kakao Map and Naver Map are nearly identical in functionality. Kakao Map is sometimes preferred by Korean people. Use whichever feels more intuitive; both work excellently.

**Why download both:** If one app crashes, you have backup navigation.

## Transportation Apps

### Subway Korea (Subway Map & Route Planner)

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** Interactive subway map with route planning and real-time information

Korea's subway system spans six major cities. This app visualizes every line, every station, and calculates fastest routes between any two points. It works offline with pre-downloaded maps.

**Best for:** Understanding the broader subway network, planning multi-day trips, navigating unfamiliar cities like Busan or Daegu.

### Kakao Taxi (Taxi Booking)

**Cost:** Free (you pay per ride, not for the app)
**What it does:** Book taxis through the app instead of hailing on the street

Kakao Taxi (also called simply "Kakao") lets you summon a taxi to your location. You see the driver's rating, can track the taxi in real-time, and fare is agreed before boarding.

**Why use it:** Avoids airport taxi overcharges and street taxi negotiations. Driver ratings provide accountability.

**Requirement:** Needs a Korean phone number to register (if you have a local SIM card). If you don't have Korean number, use Uber Taxi instead.

### Uber (Ride-Sharing)

**Cost:** Free app, pay per ride
**What it does:** Ride-sharing and taxi services in major Korean cities

Uber's ride-sharing service (called "Uber" or "Uber Black" depending on vehicle type) works in Seoul, Busan, and some other cities. More expensive than Kakao Taxi but doesn't require Korean phone registration.

**Best for:** International travelers without local phone numbers who want reliable transportation without haggling.

## Food & Restaurant Apps

### Naver Papago (Translation)

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** Real-time translation camera + text translation

Point your camera at a Korean menu and Papago translates it in real-time to your preferred language. It's not always 100% accurate, but for restaurant menus, it's approximately 85-90% reliable.

**Why it works:** Papago is made by Naver (the company behind Naver Map) and trained specifically on Korean-to-English translation.

### Google Translate

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** General-purpose translation

Google Translate works in Korea but is less reliable for specialized vocabulary (like restaurant menus). Use as backup.

### Naver (Search Engine + Reviews)

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** Korea's dominant search engine with business listings and user reviews

When looking for restaurants, shops, or attractions, Naver often has more detailed information and user reviews in Korean than Google does. An English-language version exists but is limited.

### Coupang Eats (Food Delivery)

**Cost:** Free app, delivery fees apply
**What it does:** Restaurant and food delivery service

If you need food delivered to your hotel, Coupang Eats (like a Korean Uber Eats) connects you with thousands of restaurants. Requires Korean phone registration; probably not practical for short-term visitors.

## Communication Apps

### WhatsApp or Telegram

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** Messaging over WiFi or data (doesn't use SMS)

Download before arrival. This ensures you can message family and friends back home without SMS charges. Works perfectly in Korea with any data connection.

### Kakao Talk (Korean Messaging)

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** Korea's dominant messaging app (like WhatsApp in Western countries)

If you meet Korean people and they ask "What's your Kakao Talk?" you'll understand why you need this. It's the standard communication method in Korea.

## Emergency & Safety Apps

### Emergency SOS

**Built into your phone**
In any emergency, your phone's emergency call function works. Dial 112 (police), 119 (fire/ambulance), or 1330 (tourist hotline).

**English support:** All three numbers have English-speaking operators.

### Google Maps (As Backup)

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** Street-level photography and general navigation

While Google Maps doesn't show Korean transit reliably, it's useful for finding specific stores, restaurants, and landmarks. Use it as supplement to Naver Map, not replacement.

## Fun/Cultural Apps

### Airbnb (If Using Short-Term Rentals)

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** Browse and book accommodation

Standard Airbnb interface works fine in Korea.

### Korea tourism/Travel Blogs

**Cost:** Free
**What it does:** Curated recommendations from travel bloggers

Apps like "Visit Seoul" or "Korea Tourism Organization" provide curated attractions, events, and restaurant recommendations.

## Apps to Avoid

**Google Maps (for transit only):** It works for street navigation but fail for subways and buses. Don't rely on it.

**Apps requiring Korean bank account:** Some payment apps (Toss, Kakao Pay) require Korean banking setup. Skip these as a short-term visitor.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will Google Maps work in Korea?

Partially. For walking directions and landmark locations, yes. For public transit (subways and buses), no. Transit information is incomplete or inaccurate on Google Maps in Korea. Use Naver Map or Kakao Map for any transit navigation.

### Can I download offline maps before I arrive?

Yes. For both Naver Map and Kakao Map, search "offline maps" in the app settings and download maps for any city you'll visit. This ensures navigation works even without data connection.

### Do I need to register with Korean phone number for all apps?

No. Most navigation, translation, and foreign apps (Uber, WhatsApp, Google Translate) work without Korean registration. Only Korean-specific apps like Kakao Taxi, Kakao Talk, and Coupang Eats require Korean phone numbers. As a visitor, you can navigate, eat, and travel fine without registering for those.

### Why is Naver Map better than Google Maps in Korea?

Naver Map is maintained by Naver (a Korean company) and includes every subway line, every bus route, and real-time schedules in Korea. Google doesn't maintain that level of detail for Korean transit. It's not that Google is bad globally; it's that Korean transit data is incomplete there. Naver's data is complete.
