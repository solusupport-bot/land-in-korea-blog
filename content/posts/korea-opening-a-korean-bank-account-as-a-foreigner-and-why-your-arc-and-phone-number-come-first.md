Most people arrive in Korea and treat the bank account as step one. It's closer to step three. The order that actually works is **ARC first, phone number second, bank account third** — and getting it backwards is why so many new residents spend their first month paying rent in cash and borrowing a coworker's phone for verification codes.

## Why the ARC Comes First

You generally need an Alien Registration Card before a bank will open a *fully functional* account for you. Some banks will open a limited passport-only account for short-term visitors or brand-new arrivals, and that's genuinely useful for receiving a first paycheck — but expect caps on transfers and, in many cases, no overseas remittance at all until your ARC lands.

That gap matters. If your plan is "open an account, send money home to cover a loan payment," the passport-only account may not do it. Plan for the waiting period rather than being surprised by it.

## Why the Phone Number Comes Second

A Korean phone number registered **in your own name** is what makes online banking work. Identity verification for mobile banking apps, simple-payment services and government sites all hangs off the combination of your name, your phone number and your ARC. A number registered to your employer, your landlord or a friend will verify as *them*, not you — and unwinding that later is a genuine headache.

## The Name Spelling Trap

This is the mistake that quietly ruins everything: mismatched name spellings across your passport, your phone contract and your bank records. Middle names included on one document and dropped on another, hyphens added or removed, spacing changed — any of these can break identity verification months later, when you're trying to pay a bill at 11pm.

Keep the spelling **byte-for-byte identical** across all three. Photograph your passport name page and hand it to every clerk who types your name.

## Pick the Right Branch

Branches with dedicated foreign-customer desks are worth travelling for. They cluster near university districts, industrial areas and central Seoul, and the staff there process this paperwork daily rather than once a quarter. The difference is a 30-minute appointment versus a 90-minute one with two phone calls to head office.

Bring: passport, ARC (or your ARC application receipt), proof of address or employment, and your phone.

## Sending Money Abroad

Once you pass certain annual thresholds, banks require documentation of your source of funds. A pay slip or employment contract is the usual ask. Keep digital copies somewhere you can reach them from a branch counter — not on a laptop at home.

## ATMs: The Daily Reality

Many domestic ATMs simply won't read a foreign-issued card. Look for machines marked **Global** at banks, convenience stores and airports. Also note that ATM access and withdrawal hours can be limited late at night, with extra after-hours fees on top. Plan cash withdrawals during the day rather than discovering this at 1am.

## The Practical Order

1. Apply for your ARC as soon as you're eligible.
2. Get a Korean phone number in your own legal name, spelled exactly as in your passport.
3. Open a passport-only account if you need one now — treat it as temporary.
4. Return with your ARC and upgrade to the full account.

It's slower than you want. It's much faster than doing it twice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I open a Korean bank account with just my passport?

Some banks will open a limited passport-only account for short-term visitors or new arrivals, but it may cap transfers or block overseas remittance until your ARC arrives. For a fully functional account, you generally need the ARC first.

### Do I need a Korean phone number to use mobile banking?

Yes — online banking and identity verification generally require a Korean phone number registered in your own name. Mobile banking apps, simple-payment services and government sites all verify against your name, phone number and ARC together.

### Why won't Korean ATMs accept my foreign card?

Many domestic ATMs simply don't read foreign-issued cards. Look for machines marked 'Global' at banks, convenience stores and airports, and note that access hours can be limited late at night with extra after-hours fees.
