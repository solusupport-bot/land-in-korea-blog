The washing machine isn't what catches people out when they move into a Korean apartment. It's the moment the cycle ends and they go looking for the dryer that isn't there. Most furnished studios, officetels and apartments come with a washer and nothing else, and the whole rhythm of doing laundry in Korea is built around that gap.

## The dryer you probably don't have

In most rentals, drying means a folding rack. Locals set it up in the living room or on the enclosed balcony (베란다) that many apartments have, and a load often needs most of a day to dry indoors. That changes how you plan. You don't wash a pile of towels the night before a trip, and you learn to spread loads across the week instead of doing everything on Sunday.

Two seasons make this harder. During the summer monsoon (장마), usually from late June into July, humidity is high enough that clothes dried indoors can stay damp and pick up a sour smell. Winter is the opposite problem: heating dries the air, so racks work well, but bulky sweaters and jeans still take a long time. Many households run a dehumidifier or a fan next to the rack in summer, and that's the cheapest fix if your place feels damp.

If you need things dry tonight, the coin laundry dryer is the fix, covered below.

## Decoding a Korean washing machine

The panel on a rental washer is almost always in Korean, and the default water temperature depends on the model: some drum washers heat the standard cycle, while many top-loaders use cold water unless you choose warm. Check the temperature setting (온도 or 물온도) before your first load. You only need a handful of words:

- **전원** – power. Press it first or nothing else responds.
- **동작/일시정지** – start/pause.
- **표준** – standard. The safe everyday cycle for mixed clothes.
- **이불** – bedding, for a duvet or blanket if the drum is big enough.
- **울/섬세** – wool/delicate.
- **헹굼** – rinse, and **탈수** – spin. Handy if a cycle stopped halfway or you want a second rinse.
- **온수** – hot water, on machines that offer it as a separate button.

In the detergent drawer, detergent goes in the left compartment and fabric softener goes in the one marked with a flower. Korean supermarkets sell both liquid detergent (세제) and softener (섬유유연제) in refill pouches, and the convenience store near your building usually stocks small bottles.

One building-life point: officetel walls are thin and washers on spin can be loud. Running a load late at night is one of the easier ways to annoy a neighbor, so a daytime or early-evening wash is the courteous default.

## How a coin laundry actually works

Self-service laundries, 코인빨래방, are all over residential streets and university areas. Most are unstaffed and open 24 hours, which makes them useful at odd hours but also means there's nobody to ask. The good news is that they are simpler than they look.

Machines are grouped by capacity, not by fabric type, so you pick a washer by how much you're washing. You don't need to bring detergent or softener, because both are dispensed automatically as part of the wash. Payment is often by card or mobile payment as well as coins, and in shops that still want coins there is usually a change machine on the wall.

The buttons follow the same pattern everywhere: choose wash or dry, choose a course or time, pay, start. Instructions are frequently Korean-only, but many shops post an English or picture guide, and a translation app pointed at the panel covers the rest. Some shops also have a separate sneaker washer and dryer, which is worth knowing if you've been walking in the rain all week.

The real reason to go is the dryer. Coin laundry dryers are large and hot enough to finish a full load or a duvet in one session, which no folding rack can match. Many residents wash at home and carry the damp load over just to use the dryer.

## Bedding, coats and suits: the neighborhood dry cleaner

Anything too bulky or delicate for a home machine goes to the local 세탁소. Most residential streets have one within a few minutes' walk, and prices are low by international standards. Winter padded coats, suits, blankets and wool coats are the usual items. Many 세탁소 also do basic alterations (수선) such as shortening trousers or fixing a zip, which is handy when you've bought clothes locally and the sizing is slightly off.

Many dry cleaners will pick up and drop off at your door if you call or leave a note, which is standard for older shops with a regular customer base. Keep the receipt or ticket they give you, because that's how they match your items at pickup.

There are also app-based laundry services that collect from your doorstep or a locker and return everything cleaned and folded. They're convenient if you work long hours, but they are usually built around a Korean phone number and local payment methods, so they suit residents more than short-term visitors.

## The hotel laundry trap for travelers

If you're only here for a week or two, the most expensive way to get clothes clean is the hotel laundry bag. It is priced per item, and a few shirts and a pair of trousers add up quickly.

Better options are close by. Many hostels and some mid-range hotels have a guest washer, so ask at the front desk before you hand over the bag. Otherwise, check a map app for 코인빨래방 near your accommodation. In most neighborhoods there's one within walking distance, and a single wash-and-dry for a whole suitcase costs a fraction of what a hotel charges.

## A simple plan by length of stay

- **Under two weeks:** pack for a mid-trip wash and use a coin laundry once. Skip the hotel service unless it's one urgent item.
- **One to three months:** use the in-room washer, buy a folding rack in week one, and save towels and bedding for a coin laundry run.
- **Long-term resident:** find your nearest 세탁소 for coats and bedding, learn the washer words above, and consider a dehumidifier before the monsoon starts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to bring detergent to a coin laundry in Korea?

Usually not. Most 코인빨래방 washers add detergent and softener automatically during the cycle, so all you need is your laundry and a card, phone payment or coins.

### Why don't Korean apartments have dryers?

Drying on an indoor rack or enclosed balcony has long been the norm, so most rentals are furnished with a washer only. Standalone dryers are more common in family-owned homes than in furnished studios and officetels.

### How do I get rid of the damp smell on clothes dried indoors during the monsoon?

Add an extra spin (탈수) so less water reaches the rack, hang items with space between them, and dry them in a closed room with a dehumidifier or fan running. If the sour smell has already set in, rewash the load promptly instead of masking it with more softener.
