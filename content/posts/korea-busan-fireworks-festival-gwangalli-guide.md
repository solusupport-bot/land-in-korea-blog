Once a year, Gwangalli Beach becomes the single most crowded stretch of sand in Korea. The Busan Fireworks Festival is one of the country's largest fireworks displays, set against the lit-up Gwangan Bridge, and it draws well over a million people to a beach that's normally a quiet place for coffee and a sunset walk.

This guide covers the confirmed 2026 date and details, how to actually get to Gwangalli from four places foreign visitors commonly start from, and — since getting there is only half the problem on festival night — five real, currently-open restaurants and five accommodation options nearby, all checked against live Google Maps listings rather than guessed.

## The Event, Confirmed

The 2026 edition is the **21st Busan Fireworks Festival**, and according to Korea's Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism festival listing:

- **Date:** Saturday, November 7, 2026
- **Time:** 13:00–20:00 (the fireworks themselves are an evening finale within this window, not an all-day show)
- **Location:** Gwangalli Beach, with viewing also possible from Haeundae and Igidae
- **Organizer:** Busan Metropolitan City, operated by the Busan Culture & Tourism Festival Organizing Committee
- **Official site:** [busanfireworks.com](https://busanfireworks.com/)

Admission to the beach area itself is free and this is how most people watch it — Gwangalli simply fills with a crowd. There are also **paid reserved-seating sections**; some secondary sources mention specific prices, but the organizing committee's own page only confirms seating is paid without listing a locked-in rate for 2026, so confirm the current price and availability directly on the official site or ticket platforms like NOL Ticket before planning around a specific number.

The official notice also warns that festival logistics can shift depending on the organizer's circumstances — treat the date as solid (it's on both the government listing and ticket platforms) but double-check close to your trip.

## How to Get to Gwangalli

Gwangalli doesn't have its own subway station sitting directly on the beach, but **Gwangan Station (Busan Metro Line 2)** is a short walk from the sand, and it's the station every route below ends at.

| From | Route | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Busan Station (KTX arrival) | Line 1 → transfer at Seomyeon → Line 2 | 41 min |
| Seomyeon | Line 2 direct, no transfer | 18 min |
| Haeundae | Line 2 direct, no transfer | 12 min |
| Nampo (Jagalchi/Gukje Market area) | Line 1 → transfer at Seomyeon → Line 2 | 40 min |

A few things worth knowing:

- **Seomyeon is the key transfer point** if you're not already on Line 2 — it's Busan's biggest transit hub, and both Line 1→2 routes above pass through it.
- **Haeundae to Gwangalli is a straight shot** (12 minutes, zero transfers), which makes Haeundae one of the most convenient bases if you're planning your trip around this festival specifically.
- **On festival night itself, budget extra time.** Gwangan Station gets genuinely congested as the crowd disperses after the fireworks — trains run, but platform and exit queues slow things down well beyond the normal ride time above.

## Practical Notes for the Night Itself

- **Arrive early for a spot on the sand.** Gwangalli fills up hours before the actual fireworks start, especially directly facing Gwangan Bridge.
- **The crowd is the main logistical challenge, not the event itself.** Over a million people in one beach district means slow movement everywhere — restaurants, the subway, even just walking the boardwalk.
- **Haeundae is a legitimate alternative viewing spot** if Gwangalli itself is too packed — it's named in the official listing as a secondary viewing location, and it's a straight 12-minute subway ride from Gwangan Station if you want to relocate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When exactly is the 2026 Busan Fireworks Festival?
Saturday, November 7, 2026, from 13:00 to 20:00, with the fireworks as the evening finale. It's held at Gwangalli Beach, with Haeundae and Igidae also usable as viewing spots.

### Do I need a ticket to watch?
No — most of the crowd watches for free from the beach and surrounding area. Paid reserved seating also exists; check the official site or ticket platforms for current pricing, since it isn't locked in on the government listing.

### What subway station should I use?
Gwangan Station on Busan Metro Line 2. It's a short walk from Gwangalli Beach.

### Is there a faster way in than the subway?
Not really, on festival night. Road traffic around Gwangalli gets heavy with the crowd, so the subway — despite getting congested itself — is still the more predictable option.

### Where should I stay if Gwangalli itself is fully booked?
Haeundae is the best fallback — it's a direct 12-minute subway ride on Line 2 with no transfer, and it's an official secondary viewing location for the fireworks themselves.

### Can I eat near the beach right before the show?
Yes, but expect a wait. All five restaurants above are within walking distance of Gwangalli, and festival-night demand means arriving early or being flexible on timing.
