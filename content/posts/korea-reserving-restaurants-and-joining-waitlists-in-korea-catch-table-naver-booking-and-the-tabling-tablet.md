The most common restaurant mistake visitors make in Korea isn't ordering wrong. It's showing up at 6:30 p.m. to a place with a two-hour line, or trying to phone a restaurant that stopped taking calls years ago. At popular Korean restaurants, the booking, the waitlist and even the "are you open?" question all run through apps and a tablet by the door, and almost every one of those systems assumes you have a Korean phone number.

Solo-dining rules and delivery apps have their own posts on this site. This one is about getting a table: which system a restaurant uses, what actually blocks foreigners, and what to do when you're standing outside with no Korean number.

## Three systems, three kinds of restaurant

Korean restaurants roughly sort themselves by which tool they use, and knowing the pattern saves you from searching in the wrong place.

- **Naver Place booking** is the most widespread. You find the restaurant on Naver Map, and if it takes reservations a 예약 (reserve) button appears on its listing with available time slots. Neighborhood barbecue places, brunch cafes and even hair salons use the same system, so it's worth learning once.
- **Catch Table** is where higher-end and trendy restaurants live: omakase counters, tasting menus, the places that show up on Instagram. It handles both reservations and waitlists.
- **Tabling** mostly handles waitlists at busy casual restaurants, bakeries and pop-ups that don't take reservations at all.

A quick way to tell which one a restaurant uses is to check its Naver Map listing and its Instagram profile. Restaurants usually say in their bio or pinned post where bookings open, and whether they take walk-ins at all.

## The real barrier is the phone number, not the language

Naver booking and the standard Catch Table app both generally require a Korean phone number to finish a booking, because Korean apps verify your identity through a phone line registered to your name. A foreign SIM or a data-only eSIM won't pass that step, which is why a visitor can see an open slot and still be unable to take it.

For Catch Table specifically there is a workaround. **CatchTable Global** is a separate app built for visitors: you sign up with an email address or a Google or Apple account instead of a Korean number, the interface runs in English, Japanese and Chinese, and you can pay deposits with a foreign Visa, Mastercard, AMEX or JCB card. Download the Global version by name. The domestic app looks similar but will stall at phone verification. Not every restaurant on the domestic app appears in the Global one, so if a place you want is missing, check again closer to your date or ask your hotel.

For Naver-only restaurants, the practical options are asking your hotel concierge or a Korean friend to book, or sending a message through the restaurant's Instagram. Some smaller places reply to a DM even when they won't answer the phone.

## Hyped restaurants open slots on a schedule

The most wanted tables don't trickle out. Many restaurants release a block of dates at a fixed time, often weeks ahead, and the good slots can disappear within minutes. If a restaurant matters to you:

- Find out the release day and hour (usually posted on the booking page or the restaurant's Instagram) and convert it to your home time zone if you're booking before your trip.
- Be logged in with your payment card saved a few minutes before the drop.
- Turn on the app's alert for cancellations. People release tables when plans change, especially a day or two before.

Expect a deposit at reservation-only restaurants. It exists to discourage no-shows, and the cancellation terms are shown before you pay. Read them, because late cancellations can forfeit part or all of it.

## How the tablet waitlist works

Restaurants that don't take bookings often put a tablet on a stand outside the door, usually Tabling or Catch Table Waiting. You tap in your party size and phone number, and you get an alert when your turn approaches. The point is that you don't have to stand in line: you can get a coffee or walk the block while the list moves.

Three things catch visitors out:

- **Missing the call usually means you're dropped.** You then have to register again at the back of the queue, so stay within a few minutes' walk and keep your phone volume up.
- **Alerts may not reach a foreign number.** Some queue systems send the call as a KakaoTalk message or a Korean text, and neither may arrive on an overseas line. Watch the number of teams ahead of you, which many tablets and apps display, and come back when it gets short.
- **No phone number at all?** Ask the staff to add you manually. Staff can often enter you by hand, and some tablets have a walk-in option that doesn't need a number.

CatchTable Global also lets you join the remote waitlist of participating restaurants from the app, with no deposit required for waiting, and it shows how many teams are ahead of you in real time.

## Timing: break time and last order

Plenty of Korean restaurants close their doors between lunch and dinner. This is the 브레이크타임 (break time), usually a mid-afternoon block, and it's the reason a restaurant marked "open" can have its lights half off at 3:30 p.m. Naver Map listings usually show break time next to opening hours, so check before crossing the city.

Also watch for 라스트오더 (last order), which can come well before the posted closing time. And at the most popular places, which open their waitlist at a set hour, the list for a session can fill before the doors even open, so joining right when registration starts matters more than arriving at the exact opening time.

## A plan that works without a Korean number

Install CatchTable Global before your trip and book your one or two must-eat restaurants there as soon as slots open. For everything else, pick restaurants from Naver Map, note their break time, and aim to arrive at opening or just after the lunch rush. If there's a tablet outside, register, ask the staff how long the wait is, and stay close. And when a place only books through Naver, let your hotel make the booking rather than fighting the verification screen.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use Catch Table in Korea without a Korean phone number?

Yes, through the separate CatchTable Global app, which accepts sign-up by email, Google or Apple and takes foreign credit cards for deposits. The regular domestic Catch Table app still asks for Korean phone verification.

### Do Korean restaurants charge a no-show fee or deposit?

Reservation-only restaurants on apps like Catch Table often take a deposit when you book, and late cancellation can mean losing some or all of it under the terms shown at checkout. Joining a waitlist generally costs nothing.

### What does break time mean on a Korean restaurant listing?

It is a scheduled closure between lunch and dinner service, when the kitchen preps and staff eat. Arriving during it usually means waiting outside until dinner service starts, so plan a late lunch around it.
