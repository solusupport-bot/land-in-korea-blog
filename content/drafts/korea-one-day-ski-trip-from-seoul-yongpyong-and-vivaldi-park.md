## Two resorts, two different day trips

"A day trip to the slopes from Seoul" usually means one of two places: Daemyung Vivaldi Park in Hongcheon or Yongpyong Resort in Pyeongchang, both in Gangwon province. They aren't interchangeable. Vivaldi Park sits closer to Seoul and is built around convenience — a dedicated shuttle network, a large ski school for beginners, and an attached water park (Ocean World) if anyone in your group isn't skiing at all. Yongpyong is further out but considerably larger, with more terrain and longer runs, which matters if you or anyone in your group already knows how to ski and wants more than a beginner slope to work with.

## What a shuttle-bus package actually bundles

Most of what's sold to visitors as a "ski day tour" is a bundled package: round-trip shuttle from a central Seoul pickup point, a lift ticket, and often gear rental (skis or board, boots, helmet) rolled into one price. The shuttle is the part worth paying attention to — buses typically depart from a fixed point like Hongik University Station or an area near Myeongdong, run on a seasonal schedule roughly from December through early March, and can sell out on weekends during peak snow months. Booking a package through a marketplace like [Klook]({{klook}}) bundles these pieces together and is generally easier than arranging a rental car and lift ticket separately, especially if nobody in your group has winter-driving experience on Korean mountain roads.

## Vivaldi Park: the closer, bigger-crowd option

Vivaldi Park's slopes are smaller and more beginner-oriented than Yongpyong's, which is exactly why it's the more common choice for a single-day visit — less travel time eaten up getting there, and gentler terrain for anyone skiing or snowboarding for the first time. The tradeoff is crowding: because it's the easiest resort to reach from Seoul, its beginner slopes and rental counters get busy fast on weekends, and lift lines during peak hours can eat into the time you actually spend moving.

## Yongpyong: further, but more to work with

Yongpyong was built for the 2018 PyeongChang Winter Olympics' alpine skiing events and has considerably more marked slopes and lift capacity than Vivaldi Park. The travel time from Seoul is longer, which makes it a tighter fit for a single day unless your shuttle departs early — budget for the extra travel by starting earlier than you think you need to, since the actual skiing window on a day trip shrinks fast once transit time on both ends is subtracted from daylight hours.

## Rental gear — what's included and what isn't

Package listings usually specify whether ski/board, boots, and outerwear (jacket and pants) are included or rented separately at the resort. Helmets are increasingly included by default but worth confirming, especially for children. Thermal base layers and gloves are rarely included in any package — pack your own or expect to buy them at the resort, where prices run higher than in Seoul.

## Realistic timing for a single day

Between the shuttle ride each way, check-in and gear fitting, and the return trip, a single-day package typically leaves four to six hours of actual slope time, less at Yongpyong given the longer drive. That's enough for a first-timer to get through a beginner lesson and a few runs, but not enough to meaningfully progress past the basics or explore much of a larger resort like Yongpyong. If skiing is the main point of the trip rather than a one-off experience, an overnight package gets considerably more value out of the travel time than a single day does.

## Booking ahead vs. walking up

Weekday shuttles have more availability and shorter lift lines than weekend departures, and booking at least a few days ahead is worth it during January and February, when demand peaks around school holidays. Walking up to a resort without a booked shuttle is possible if you're driving or taking public transit independently, but check the resort's own seasonal operating calendar first, since both resorts open and close around the snowfall rather than on fixed calendar dates.

## Getting there without a package

A shuttle package isn't the only route. KTX service runs from Seoul to Gangneung and Jinbu stations in Gangwon, both reasonably close to Yongpyong, with a local taxi or resort shuttle covering the final stretch — a workable option if your schedule doesn't match the tour shuttle's fixed departure time, though it generally costs more once the train fare and taxi are added up separately. Vivaldi Park has no direct KTX station nearby, which is part of why its bundled shuttle packages are the dominant way visitors reach it rather than one option among several.

## Who this doesn't work for

If anyone in your group has never skied and is nervous about a fast-paced day, the shuttle-and-return structure of a day trip adds pressure that an overnight stay doesn't. The same goes for anyone prone to motion sickness on winding mountain roads — the drive to either resort, especially Yongpyong, involves real elevation change. For a more relaxed first experience, Vivaldi Park's shorter transit time and gentler slopes are the safer starting point.
