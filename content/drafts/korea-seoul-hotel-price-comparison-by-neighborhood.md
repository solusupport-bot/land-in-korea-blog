"Where should I stay in Seoul" is really two questions: which neighborhood fits your trip, and what will it actually cost there. These two things don't move together the way you'd expect — the cheapest areas aren't always the least convenient, and the most famous tourist neighborhood isn't the most expensive.

**A note on the numbers below:** these are ranges reported by hotel-aggregator sites and travel blogs, accessed 2026-10-31, not live rates we checked ourselves, and the sources don't fully agree with each other — lodging prices move constantly with season, day of week, and demand. Use this to understand relative positioning between neighborhoods, then check live rates for your actual travel dates before booking.

## The four neighborhoods, compared

| Neighborhood | Reported nightly range (USD) | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Dongdaemun | ~$62 average (cheapest in Seoul per one aggregator) | Budget stays, late-night shopping/market access |
| Hongdae | ~$70 average; hostels from ~$30 | First-timers on a budget, nightlife, younger crowd |
| Myeongdong | Hostels $45-70, mid-range $95-140, luxury $200+ | Central tourist base, shopping, easy transit |
| Gangnam | ~$83 average; specific properties from ~₩120,000 | Business travel, upscale dining, nightlife with a different crowd than Hongdae |

One aggregator (momondo) puts the overall Seoul average around $89/night, with Hongdae running about 21% below that average and Dongdaemun as the single cheapest district it tracked. A different source (Travorio) shows Myeongdong itself spanning the widest range of any neighborhood — from $45 hostels up past $200 for luxury properties — because Myeongdong has the densest concentration of hotel types of any district on this list.

## What you're actually trading off

**Dongdaemun — cheapest, but check why before booking.** The lower average price partly reflects a market/wholesale-district character rather than a polished tourist strip. It's genuinely convenient if late-night shopping at Dongdaemun Design Plaza or the markets there is part of your plan; less so if you want to walk out of your hotel straight into sightseeing.

**Hongdae — the best budget pick for first-timers.** Cheaper than the Seoul average, dense with hostels and guesthouses from around $30, and walkable to a genuinely lively nightlife and café district aimed at a younger crowd (university-area energy, live-music bars, street performers on weekends). The tradeoff is noise — if you want quiet evenings, the entertainment that makes Hongdae appealing at night is the same thing working against you at 1 AM.

**Myeongdong — the default choice for a reason.** It sits in the middle to upper range depending on which specific property you pick, but its real value is location: walking distance to [Namdaemun Market](https://landinkorea.com/posts/korea-namdaemun-market.html), a straightforward [T-money](https://landinkorea.com/posts/korea-t-money-transit-card.html) subway connection to most major attractions, and dense duty-free and skincare shopping right outside your door. If this is a first trip and you don't want to think hard about logistics, Myeongdong removes most of that thinking.

**Gangnam — upscale, but not "expensive tourist trap" upscale.** Reported averages ($83-120,000+ KRW) sit close to Seoul's overall average, not dramatically above it — the premium you're paying is more about access to Gangnam's restaurant and nightlife scene (different in character from Hongdae — more upscale bars and dining, less street-performance energy) than purely about the hotel itself.

## A simple way to decide

- **First trip, don't want to overthink logistics:** Myeongdong.
- **Tightest budget, comfortable with hostel-style stays:** Hongdae.
- **Want the absolute lowest number and don't mind a less touristy base:** Dongdaemun.
- **Business trip or want Seoul's upscale dining/nightlife scene:** Gangnam.

## Booking timing

None of the sources above are live, checked-today rates — hotel pricing shifts constantly with season and specific dates. The practical move: use the neighborhood comparison above to pick where you want to be, then check live rates on your actual booking platform of choice for your specific travel dates before committing. A neighborhood that looks mid-range in October can price very differently during cherry blossom season or a major holiday week.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Myeongdong too touristy to be worth the price?

It's touristy, but that's largely the point for a first trip — you're paying for proximity to the things first-timers actually want to see, not for a hidden-gem experience. If you want fewer tourists, Hongdae or a quieter Gangnam side-street is a better fit than paying less in a neighborhood further from the sights.

### Which neighborhood has the easiest subway access?

All four sit on or near Seoul's subway network, so this isn't a major differentiator — Myeongdong and Gangnam both sit directly on major lines with minimal walking to the station, which is the main practical edge they have over Hongdae and Dongdaemun for someone changing hotels or day-tripping often.

### Should I split my stay across two neighborhoods?

For trips over 5-6 days, it's a reasonable way to see more of the city without long commutes — for example, a few nights in Myeongdong for the central sights, then a few in Hongdae or Gangnam for a different pace. For shorter trips, picking one well-located base (Myeongdong is the safest default) usually beats the time lost to moving hotels.

### Are these prices before or after tax?

Not specified consistently across our sources — Korean hotels commonly add VAT and sometimes a service charge to the quoted room rate. Confirm whether the rate you're booking is final or before tax at checkout, since this can meaningfully change the total versus what you compared above.
