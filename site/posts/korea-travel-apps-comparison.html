"Google Maps vs. Naver Map vs. KakaoMap: Which Actually Works in Korea"

Google Maps works fine for getting to Korea. It's noticeably weaker once you're actually navigating inside it — Google's local transit and walking-direction data in Korea has real gaps that most first-timers don't discover until they're already lost.

Why Google Maps is worse in Korea specifically

This isn't Google being lazy about one market — Korea has long restricted detailed mapping data from being stored on servers outside the country for security reasons, and Google hasn't been granted the exception it has in most other countries. That's the practical reason driving directions, some points of interest, and offline map downloads are all noticeably thinner in Korea than what you're used to from Google Maps at home. It's a structural gap, not something that's likely to quietly fix itself mid-trip.

Naver Map vs. KakaoMap: pick either, both beat Google here

Both apps show real-time subway and bus arrival times, platform-level transfer directions inside stations, and walking routes that actually match Korean street data. Most visitors don't discover the platform-level transfer directions until well into their trip — it's the single most useful feature neither Google Maps nor most people's home country app has an equivalent for. Naver Map also integrates local restaurant review data that tends to be more current than international review apps.

Both apps support switching the interface to English in their settings — do this on first launch rather than trying to navigate Korean menus under pressure. One honest caveat: even with the interface in English, smaller businesses and some points of interest may still only display their Korean name, so having Papago's camera translation as a backup for reading a storefront sign is worth keeping in your back pocket. Neither app offers offline map downloads as complete as Google Maps does in other countries, so a working data connection matters more here than travelers used to downloading an offline map area might expect.

Papago, for everything language-related

Papago handles Korean translation more naturally than general-purpose translation apps for this specific context. It's worth knowing it does two genuinely different things: camera translation for menus and street signs, and a separate voice conversation mode for back-and-forth spoken exchanges — useful for asking a question at a pharmacy counter or a ticket window, not just reading text.

Kakao T, for taxis (and more)

Kakao T is the go-to app for calling a taxi reliably in Korea, especially late at night or in areas where flagging one down from the street is difficult. Fares are estimated upfront in the app before you confirm. It's grown beyond just taxis too — the same app offers bike-share rentals in supported cities, and a "designated driver" service that sends a driver to drive your own car home for you after drinking, which is a distinctly Korean service concept worth knowing about even if it's more relevant to foreign residents with a car than to short-term tourists.

The one setup step people skip

Download Naver Map, Papago, and Kakao T before you land, and switch the map apps to English in settings while you're at it — some functions work more reliably with a local IP or an active Korean SIM/eSIM, and fumbling with app store downloads and language settings on spotty airport Wi-Fi is a bad first impression of a trip. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Google Maps work well in Korea?

Not really — it has real gaps in local transit and walking-direction data due to long-standing restrictions on mapping data being stored outside Korea, and most first-timers don't discover this until they're already lost.

Which map app should I use instead in Korea?

Naver Map or KakaoMap — both show real-time subway and bus arrivals, platform-level transfer directions, and walking routes that match Korean street data. Switch either to English in the settings before you land.

Can I download offline maps for Korea the way I do elsewhere with Google Maps?

Not to the same extent — neither Naver Map nor KakaoMap offers offline downloads as complete as Google Maps provides in other countries, so a reliable data connection matters more here.

What's the difference between Papago's camera translation and its conversation mode?

Camera translation reads text like menus or signs through your phone's camera. Conversation mode is for spoken back-and-forth, useful when you need to actually ask someone a question rather than just read something.

What app should I use for taxis in Korea?

Kakao T — it's the go-to app for reliably calling a taxi, with fares estimated upfront before you confirm. It also covers bike-share and a designated-driver service in supported areas.

Should I download these apps before or after I land in Korea?

Before — some functions work more reliably with a local IP or an active Korean SIM/eSIM, and downloading apps plus switching language settings on unfamiliar airport Wi-Fi right after a long flight isn't worth the hassle.
