Nami Island and Petite France: A Full Day Trip Guide

By Land in Korea Desk · Editorial policy

The island you can't just drive onto

Nami Island isn't connected to the mainland by any bridge — it's a genuine half-moon-shaped island in the Han River near Gapyeong, and the only ways across are a short ferry ride (about five minutes) or a zipline that drops you near the entrance from the opposite bank. First-timers who assume there's road access occasionally show up at the wrong riverside with a rental car and no way over. The ferry runs frequently and doesn't need advance booking on quiet days, but it does pause in high wind or fog, so leave slack if you're catching a train back to Seoul the same evening.

The "Naminara Republic" ticket is a running joke, not a scam

Nami Island declared itself an independent micro-nation called the Naminara Republic in 2006, complete with its own flag, currency, postage stamps, and a citizenship program. It's branding, not an actual border crossing, but it shapes how tickets are sold: standard admission is officially labeled an "entry visa," priced at 19,000 won for a regular adult ticket per Korea Tourism Organization listings, and that price already bundles the round-trip ferry fare — there's no separate boat charge. Frequent visitors can buy an annual "passport" instead of a new visa each visit. You don't need a real passport at the gate; the paperwork theme is just for the souvenir stamps and photos at the entrance booth.

Getting to Gapyeong from Seoul

The ITX-Cheongchun train runs from Yongsan or Cheongnyangni station to Gapyeong Station in about an hour. From there, a taxi to the ferry pier takes five to ten minutes and runs roughly 5,000-6,000 won, or you can catch local bus 10-4, which connects the station to the terminal — a T-money card covers the fare either way. If you'd rather skip the transfers, direct round-trip shuttle buses run daily from spots like Hongdae, Myeongdong, and Dongdaemun straight to the ferry dock, though they need an online reservation and only run as round-trip packages.

Skipping the ferry for the zipline

Nami Island also has a zipline that carries riders from the mainland down to a landing point near the entrance — a one-way novelty for the adrenaline crowd. It's a separate paid attraction, runs on its own weather-dependent schedule, and only gets you there, not back, so you'll still take the ferry out. Booking ahead in peak season helps since capacity is limited compared to the ferry's continuous runs.

Why everyone actually comes here

The island's tree-lined paths — especially the ginkgo and metasequoia lanes — were the filming location for the 2002 KBS drama Winter Sonata, one of the shows credited with kicking off the broader wave of Korean pop-culture tourism among Japanese and Taiwanese visitors in the early 2000s. Decades later, those same paths remain the main draw and one of the most photographed spots in Korea in any season, whether it's yellow ginkgo leaves in late autumn or the metasequoia trees' straight-line perspective the rest of the year.

What Petite France actually is

Petite France is a French-village-themed park built in 2008 in Gapyeong, a short taxi or shuttle ride from the Nami Island ferry pier rather than something reachable on foot. It's built around the theme of "flowers, stars, and the Little Prince," with 16 French-style buildings that include a memorial hall to Antoine de Saint-Exupéry, plus scheduled marionette and music box performances through the day. It's also a filming location for the drama My Love from the Star, which is part of why it draws its own dedicated visitors independent of Nami Island next door.

Why almost nobody does this as two separate trips

Public transport between Nami Island, Petite France, the Garden of Morning Calm, and the Gangchon Rail Bike isn't direct, so most visitors book a combined day tour rather than arranging each transfer themselves. These are commonly sold as four-in-one packages running roughly 12 to 13 hours door-to-door from Seoul. It's possible to string the stops together independently with taxis and local buses, but the packaged tours exist because doing it yourself eats a large chunk of the day in transfers alone.

When to go and how to budget your day

Autumn foliage season and spring are the busiest stretches for both Nami Island and Petite France; a weekday visit noticeably thins the crowds along the main paths. Round-trip travel from central Seoul runs about one to one and a half hours each way before you factor in transfers between attractions, which is why this works better as a full-day plan than a quick add-on to another Seoul itinerary.

The short version

  • Nami Island is a true island — only reachable by ferry or a one-way zipline, no bridge
  • Admission is officially an "entry visa" (~19,000 won) that already includes the round-trip ferry
  • Get to Gapyeong via the ITX-Cheongchun train (~1 hour from Yongsan/Cheongnyangni), then taxi or bus 10-4 to the pier
  • Direct Seoul shuttle buses to the ferry dock exist but need online reservation
  • The tree-lined paths are famous because of Winter Sonata, filmed here in 2002
  • Petite France is a separate paid attraction nearby, themed around The Little Prince
  • Most visitors combine both with Garden of Morning Calm and the Gangchon Rail Bike as a single day tour, since transit between them isn't direct
  • Autumn and spring are the busiest seasons — go on a weekday if you can

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa or passport to enter Nami Island?

No. The admission ticket is jokingly branded an "entry visa" as part of the island's self-declared Naminara Republic theme, but it's a regular ticket — no real passport or ID beyond payment is required.

Does the Nami Island ticket include the ferry?

Yes. The standard admission price (around 19,000 won) bundles the round-trip ferry fare, so there's no separate boat ticket to buy at the pier.

Can I drive or walk directly to Nami Island?

No. There's no bridge — the only ways in are the ferry (about five minutes) or the one-way zipline from the mainland, which still requires the ferry to get back.

How do I get from Seoul to Nami Island without a tour?

Take the ITX-Cheongchun train from Yongsan or Cheongnyangni to Gapyeong Station (about an hour), then a taxi or local bus 10-4 to the ferry pier. A direct reserved shuttle bus from Hongdae or Myeongdong is also an option.

Are Nami Island and Petite France within walking distance of each other?

No. They're separate sites near Gapyeong connected by taxi, local bus, or tour shuttle.

Why is Nami Island so popular with tourists?

Its ginkgo and metasequoia tree-lined paths were the filming location for the 2002 drama Winter Sonata, which helped drive the early wave of Korean drama tourism and remains the island's main draw today.
