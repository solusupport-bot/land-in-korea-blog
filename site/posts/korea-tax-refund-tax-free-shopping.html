The mistake that costs people their refund

Most tourists lose their Tax Free refund before they even reach the airport — not from missing paperwork, but from where they packed the item. The refund process at Incheon and Gimpo requires customs to see and stamp the actual goods before you check your bag, so if you toss the tax-free purchase into checked luggage and drop it at a normal counter, there's nothing left to inspect. The fix is simple: keep any Tax Free item accessible until it's been through the customs step, and only check the bag afterward.

How the refund actually works

Tourists who spend over the minimum threshold at Tax Free-affiliated stores can reclaim the VAT paid on the purchase. Korea's official tourism site lists a baseline of 15,000 won per receipt to qualify, though thresholds and refund percentages are adjusted periodically, so check the current numbers before you shop. At checkout, show your passport and ask for a Tax Free receipt or form — this is a separate document from the regular receipt, and stores won't issue it after the fact. You also have to leave Korea within three months of the purchase date for that receipt to stay valid.

Not every 'tax free' sign means the same thing

Multiple companies operate Korea's tax refund system — Global Blue, Korea Tax Free, Global Tax Free and others — and each runs its own kiosk or counter at the airport. Match the logo on your receipt to the right kiosk; a Global Blue receipt won't process at a Korea Tax Free machine. Stores display a sticker near the entrance or register showing which network they belong to, and a 'tax free prices' sign without that sticker usually just means duty-free-adjacent pricing, not an actual VAT refund. Check for the sticker shop by shop if you're browsing around Myeongdong or Namdaemun Market rather than assuming the whole district is covered.

Getting the refund instantly, at the register

Certified stores — mostly department stores, large supermarkets, and some convenience chains — can deduct the tax on the spot instead of making you wait until the airport. This only applies within limits: a single transaction generally has to fall between 15,000 and 1,000,000 won, and total tax-free spending across your trip is capped around 5,000,000 won. Go over either limit and that purchase falls back to the standard paper-receipt process. You still need your passport at checkout, and the item still has to leave the country unused.

The airport process, step by step

If your Tax Free items are going in checked luggage, don't check that bag first. The sequence is: check in for your flight but hold onto the baggage, take the goods and receipts to the customs desk near check-in for an export stamp, then bring the stamped bag to the oversized baggage counter to actually check it. After security and immigration, find the refund counter or self-service kiosk matching your receipt's operator, scan your passport and forms, and collect the refund in cash or to a card. Kiosks back up fast during morning departure waves, so build in extra time — and if you haven't sorted transport yet, see airport transfer options so you're not rushing the whole sequence.

If the counters are already closed

Land at the airport late and find the refund desks shuttered, and you're not automatically out of luck. Korea's tourism authorities note that travelers can drop stamped receipts into designated deposit boxes near the counters, with the refund transferred to an international credit card, Alipay, or WeChat Pay, typically within one to two months. It's a fallback, not a first choice — slower, and dependent on the box being processed correctly — but it beats losing the refund outright over a tight boarding call.

Cards, cash, and what actually lands back in your account

Whichever way you get refunded, don't expect the full VAT rate back. Refund operators deduct their own administrative fee from what you're owed, so the amount that clears is noticeably less than the sticker-price tax rate — plan around that rather than treating it as extra spending money. It's worth also knowing how card payments and currency exchange work in Korea, since the refund itself usually posts back to whatever card you used or selected at the kiosk.

The short version

  • Keep tax-free items out of checked luggage until customs has seen and stamped them
  • Minimum purchase to qualify is a small threshold (officially 15,000 won per receipt) — verify current numbers before shopping
  • Ask for the Tax Free receipt at checkout and show your passport; it can't be issued later
  • Match your receipt's logo (Global Blue, Korea Tax Free, etc.) to the correct airport kiosk
  • Instant in-store refunds exist but are capped per transaction and per trip
  • Airport order matters: customs stamp before checking your bag, kiosk/counter after security
  • Missed the counter? Stamped receipts can go in a deposit box for a delayed refund
  • Expect less than the full VAT rate back once administrative fees are deducted
  • You must leave Korea within three months of the purchase date for the receipt to count

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get an instant tax refund instead of waiting until the airport?

Yes, at certified stores that deduct the tax at checkout, but only within limits — a single transaction generally needs to fall between 15,000 and 1,000,000 won, with trip-wide spending capped around 5,000,000 won.

Do I need to show the actual item at customs, or just the receipt?

The actual goods, available for inspection — that's why packing a tax-free purchase into checked luggage before the customs step is the most common mistake.

What happens if the airport refund counter is already closed when I arrive?

You can drop your stamped receipts into a designated deposit box near the counters, and the refund is transferred to an international credit card, Alipay, or WeChat Pay, usually within one to two months.

Why did my refund come back lower than 10% of what I spent?

Refund operators deduct their own administrative fee from the VAT amount before paying out, so the cash or card refund is less than the full tax rate printed on the receipt.

Does it matter which company's logo is on my tax-free receipt?

Yes. Global Blue, Korea Tax Free, and Global Tax Free each run separate kiosks, and a receipt from one operator won't process at another's machine, so match the logo before you queue.

Is there a deadline for using my tax-free receipt after I buy something?

Yes — you need to leave Korea within three months of the purchase date for the receipt to remain valid for a refund.
