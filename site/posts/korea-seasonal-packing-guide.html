The Korea Packing Mistake That Has Nothing to Do With the Weather Outside

Most packing advice for Korea focuses on the season outside: hot, humid summers with a July monsoon; dry, cold winters with occasional heavy snow outside Seoul; comfortable but crowded spring and fall. That's real, and worth planning around — but it's not what actually catches people off guard.

The real problem: indoor heating and AC run stronger than you expect

Korean buildings run heating and air conditioning noticeably stronger than most visitors are used to. In winter, that means freezing streets and overheated subways and stores in the same 20-minute walk. In summer, it's humid streets and aggressively cold AC indoors. A packing list built only around the season outside leaves you either sweating indoors or shivering on transit, regardless of which season you're visiting in.

The fix: layers you can actually remove, not just warm layers

Prioritize clothing you can peel off and stuff in a bag over clothing that's simply "warm enough" — a base layer plus a removable mid-layer beats one thick coat for moving between outdoor cold and indoor heat repeatedly in a single day.

Footwear matters more than most itineraries account for

Korean cities involve a lot of walking and stairs, and not every subway station has elevators. Comfortable walking shoes matter more than what you'd normally pack for a city trip elsewhere.

If you're traveling during monsoon season (July)

Quick-dry fabric beats cotton, both for comfort and for not smelling faintly of a sudden downpour for the rest of the day. A compact umbrella is more useful than a heavy rain jacket for the short, intense bursts typical of Korean summer rain.
