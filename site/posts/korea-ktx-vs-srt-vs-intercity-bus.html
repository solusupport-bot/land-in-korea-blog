KTX vs SRT vs Intercity Bus in Korea - Which to Pick

Written by Land in Korea Desk · checked against official sources — see how we work

Traveling between Korean cities requires choosing between three options: KTX (high-speed rail), SRT (semi-high-speed rail), or intercity bus. Each has different speeds, costs, and comfort levels. This guide breaks down the comparison and recommends which to use based on your priorities.

Option 1: KTX (고속철도 - Korea Train Express)

Speed: Fastest option. Seoul to Busan in 2.5 hours.

Cost: Check current fares on the Korail app or website — pricing varies by distance, time of booking, and whether you qualify for a discount fare.

Comfort: Most comfortable. Assigned seats, spacious legroom, smooth ride, first-class and economy options.

Reliability: 99%+ on-time performance. Trains run every 15-30 minutes on major routes.

Routes covered: Seoul ↔ Busan, Seoul ↔ Gwangju, Seoul ↔ Daegu, and many others. Connects most major Korean cities.

Booking: Buy at Seoul Station ticket counters, online via Korail website, or through travel apps.

Seats: Can be reserved in advance. Refund fees increase the closer you get to departure time, so check the fee schedule if your plans might change.

Luggage: Each passenger allowed 2 pieces of luggage (within size limits). Overhead racks and luggage racks available.

Experience: Quiet, professional, modern trains. Popular with Korean businesspeople. Bringing your own snacks and drinks is normal; some trains also have a cafe car.

Best for: Long-distance travel (4+ hours), time-sensitive trips, comfort priority.

Option 2: SRT (수서고속철도 - Seoul-Busan High-Speed Rail)

Speed: Very close to KTX on shared routes like Seoul-Busan — often within a few minutes of the same total travel time, since both run on the same high-speed track for most of the journey.

Cost: Typically a bit cheaper than KTX for the same route — check current fares on the Korail or SRT/Railigo app, since pricing changes with season and how far ahead you book.

Comfort: Nearly identical to KTX. Assigned seats, spacious legroom, smooth ride.

Reliability: 99%+ on-time. Runs every 20-40 minutes.

Routes: Mainly Seoul to Busan, Seoul to Daegu, and limited other routes. Fewer options than KTX.

Booking: Through Korail app, Railigo website, or Seoul Station (separate ticket counters from KTX).

Key difference from KTX: Departs from Suseo Station, not Seoul Station — Suseo is in southern Seoul (Gangnam area) and is a completely different station from where KTX leaves. This trips up a lot of first-time bookers who show up at Seoul Station with an SRT ticket.

Best for: Budget-conscious travelers, same destinations as KTX but wanting slightly lower fares.

Option 3: Intercity Bus (고속버스 - Express Bus)

Speed: Slowest option. Seoul to Busan in 4-5 hours (includes one stop). Comparable to SRT if you account for bus station travel time.

Cost: Cheapest. 25,000-35,000 won for Seoul-Busan. Nearly half KTX price.

Comfort: Adequate but less comfortable than trains. Seats are smaller, less legroom, bus-like experience (bumpy at times).

Reliability: Good, but traffic-dependent. Delays possible during rush hours or accidents.

Routes: Most extensive network. Connects nearly every Korean city and small towns. Better for regional/small-town travel.

Booking: At bus terminals, online via various websites, or through travel apps. Easier walk-up availability than KTX.

Luggage: Stored in bus underbody. One carry-on + checked bag included.

Rest stop: Most long-distance buses stop once (15-30 minutes) for bathroom/food break.

Accessibility: Buses serve small towns KTX doesn't reach. Good for rural exploration.

Best for: Budget travelers, regional/small-town routes, flexibility in timing.

Which to Choose: Decision Matrix

Choose KTX if:

  • You value speed/time most
  • You're traveling long-distance (4+ hours)
  • You want premium comfort
  • You prefer reliability
  • You have luggage

Choose SRT if:

  • You want KTX quality at slightly lower cost
  • The route serves your destination
  • You're not in extreme hurry

Choose intercity bus if:

  • Budget is priority
  • You're traveling to small towns
  • You have flexibility on timing
  • You want casual experience
  • You want frequent departures with walk-up availability

Booking Tips

Book in advance for:

  • Friday/Saturday/Sunday (high demand)
  • Holidays and peak season (spring/fall)
  • Specific time preferences

Walk-up works for:

  • Weekday travel
  • Off-season
  • Flexible timing

Price differences:

  • Off-peak travel (weekday morning): cheapest
  • Weekend travel: 10-20% markup
  • Holiday travel: 20-30% markup

Refunds:

  • KTX/SRT: Refund fees increase the closer to departure time — check the fee schedule on the app before assuming a full refund
  • Bus: Varies by operator; generally refundable 1-2 hours before

Frequently Asked Questions

Is KTX really worth the extra money vs. bus?

For 4-hour journeys, yes. You save 2+ hours and gain comfort. The 22,000₩ difference ($16-17) is worth 2 hours of your time. For 2-hour routes, maybe not—buses are less practical for short distances.

Can I bring luggage on KTX?

Yes, 2 pieces per person without surcharge. Size limits apply (roughly 80×60×40 cm each). Larger luggage requires additional payment or storage service.

Is SRT different from KTX?

Minor differences only. Same comfort level, slightly slower, slightly cheaper. Routes are more limited for SRT. Choose based on departure time and price.
