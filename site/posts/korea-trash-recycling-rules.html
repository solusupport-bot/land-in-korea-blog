Korea's Trash and Recycling Rules Tourists Must Follow

Written by Land in Korea Desk · checked against official sources — see how we work

Korea has one of the world's most rigorous trash sorting and recycling systems. The rules are detailed, enforced, and violations result in fines (up to 1 million won/$750 USD).

As a tourist, you might throw trash carelessly without realizing it's illegal. This guide explains Korea's trash system and how to follow the rules.

Why Korea Is So Strict on Trash

Korea is small (100 million people in area smaller than Pennsylvania) with minimal landfill space. Everything must be recycled or incinerated. The system is designed to minimize waste.

The fines are real. Korean residents get fined regularly for improper sorting. Tourists are technically exempt from fines, but respecting local norms is important.

General Trash Rules

Rule 1: Don't throw trash in public Unlike many countries, there are virtually no public trash cans in Korea. Koreans carry trash home and sort it there. Tourists are sometimes allowed to leave trash at convenience stores, but it's technically not your trash can.

Rule 2: Never throw trash on the street This is littering and illegal. Fines apply.

Rule 3: Respect building trash disposal times Each apartment building has designated collection times (usually 7-9 PM or early morning). Trash outside those times is illegal.

Rule 4: Separate trash into categories Trash isn't just trash. It must be categorized: food waste, recycling, general waste, etc.

Trash Categories

Trash in Korea separates into four main categories:

1. Food Waste (음식물쓰레기)

What goes in:

  • Vegetable scraps
  • Fruit peels
  • Meat/bone leftovers
  • Leftover cooked food
  • Coffee grounds
  • Tea bags
  • Any organic food matter

What doesn't:

  • Plastic, glass, metal
  • Paper (even food-contaminated paper)
  • Non-food items

Disposal:

  • Use designated food waste bin (usually dark/colored)
  • Drain excess liquid before throwing (food waste is by weight; liquid adds cost)
  • Food waste is collected separately and composted

2. Recycling (재활용)

Subcategories:

Paper/Cardboard (종이)

  • Newspaper, magazines, books
  • Cardboard boxes
  • Junk mail
  • MUST flatten cardboard and bundle with string
  • Remove all plastic, tape, stickers

Plastic/PET (플라스틱/PET)

  • Plastic bottles, containers
  • Yogurt containers
  • Plastic bags
  • MUST rinse and dry
  • Remove labels if possible
  • Plastic bags go in their own category sometimes

Glass (유리병)

  • Beer bottles, wine bottles
  • Clear glass bottles
  • Brown/green glass bottles (separated sometimes)
  • MUST NOT be broken
  • Keep lids off
  • Rinse thoroughly

Metal/Aluminum (캔/금속)

  • Aluminum cans
  • Steel cans
  • Metal containers
  • MUST be rinsed and drained
  • Keep lids on

3. General Waste (일반쓰레기)

What goes in:

  • Styrofoam/polystyrene
  • Non-recyclable plastics
  • Ceramics
  • Certain packaging materials
  • Anything not in other categories

Disposal:

  • Use designated general waste bin
  • Usually has different color/label than others

4. Large Items (대형폐기물)

What qualifies:

  • Furniture
  • Electronics (sometimes separate)
  • Large broken items
  • Appliances

Disposal process:

  • Cannot throw with regular trash
  • Must contact building management or waste collection service
  • May require special sticker or collection appointment
  • Tourists should contact hotel if staying during departure

Practical Trash Disposal for Tourists

In Hotels

Ask your hotel for trash sorting instructions. Provide bags for different categories, and leave sorted trash. Staff will handle final disposal.

In Apartments/Airbnb

ASK your host about sorting rules before producing trash. Different buildings have different systems. Your host will explain:

  • Where trash goes
  • What hours to leave it
  • How to sort
  • Where bins are located

In Convenience Stores

Convenience stores (GS25, CU, Emart24) sometimes allow you to leave food waste from purchases. Example: You buy a gimbap (Korean sushi) and finish it at the store—toss the container in their food waste bin.

Unspoken rule: Only throw away what you bought there. Don't bring outside trash.

Street Level

There are NO public trash cans. Carry trash until you reach your hotel, apartment, or convenience store. This is normal behavior for Koreans.

Common Tourist Mistakes

Mistake 1: Throwing trash on the street or in subway Illegal and subject to fines (100,000-1 million won depending on severity).

Mistake 2: Not separating trash If your trash gets mixed (food waste with plastic), your host or hotel will have to separate it. Respectfully rude.

Mistake 3: Throwing wet/liquid trash in dry waste bin Food waste must go in food waste. Wet trash in general waste causes problems.

Mistake 4: Throwing trash in random bins Random bin throwing (even if you mean well) might be illegal sorting violation.

Mistake 5: Leaving trash outside designated times Building trash collection times are strict (usually 7-9 PM). Trash left outside those times is illegal (yes, really).

What Happens If You Violate Rules

For tourists: Tourists aren't usually fined, but repeat violations might result in complaints to your accommodation provider or police involvement for severe violations (littering in public).

For residents: Fines range 100,000-1 million won (serious violations).

Simple Rules for Tourist Compliance

  1. Carry trash until hotel. There are no public bins. This is normal.
  2. Ask your host about sorting. Each location might differ slightly.
  3. Separate food waste from other trash. This is the main rule.
  4. Rinse recyclables. Food residue causes problems.
  5. Don't litter. It's illegal and disrespectful.
  6. Respect collection times. Leave trash only when building/residence allows.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why doesn't Korea have public trash cans?

Korea deliberately removed public trash cans in the 1990s to enforce home sorting. It worked—Koreans now automatically carry trash home. The system maximizes recycling rates to 80%+ (among world's highest).

Will I get fined for improper trash if I'm a tourist?

Unlikely. Fines are generally for residents. But locals will judge you. Respecting rules shows cultural awareness.

How do I dispose of large items like suitcases?

Talk to your hotel. They handle large item disposal. Don't try to throw it in building trash.
