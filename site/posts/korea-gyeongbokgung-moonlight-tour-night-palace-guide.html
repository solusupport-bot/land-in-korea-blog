Most visitors see Gyeongbokgung Palace in the middle of a bright, crowded afternoon. For a few weeks each autumn, the palace also opens after dark for a ticketed night program, and the experience is a completely different one — empty courtyards, lit-up rooflines, and none of the daytime tour-group noise.

This guide covers the exact dates and times, what it actually costs, how to book it if you're not a Korean resident, subway directions from five tourist hubs, and where to stay nearby.

The 2026 season has ended. This program ran through October 24, 2026, and ticket sales are closed. Gyeongbokgung is still open for regular daytime and general night viewing — see the schedule below for reference, or jump straight to Bukchon Hanok Village and Insadong, both walkable from the palace, for what to do right now.

The Schedule, Confirmed

According to the Korea Heritage Service (국가유산진흥원), the 2026 program — officially called Gyeongbokgung Byeolbit-yahaeng (경복궁 별빛야행, "Starlight Night Walk") — runs as follows:

  • Dates: September 2 – October 24, 2026
  • Days: Wednesday through Sunday only (closed Monday and Tuesday)
  • No operation: September 30 – October 4 (reserved for a separate lantern festival)
  • Sessions: Two per operating night — 6:20–8:10 PM and 7:30–9:20 PM, each about 110 minutes
  • Capacity: 38 people per session — small, and it sells out

Price and How to Book

Price is 60,000 KRW per person, and includes a guided route through the palace plus a traditional palace-food tasting (doseok surasang). This is a different, more expensive product than Gyeongbokgung's regular evening opening hours (a separate general night viewing option, priced around 3,000 KRW, with no guide and no food).

General admission runs through TicketLink (ticketlink.co.kr) as a lottery, not first-come-first-served — you apply for a chance to buy, then get a short payment window if selected. The main lottery application already closed in mid-August, so if you're reading this now, don't go looking for an "apply" button on TicketLink expecting an open entry — for most of the run, your only realistic options there are checking for last-minute cancellations or a resale listing.

The one booking path still genuinely open to tourists is the foreigner-only window on the final weekend, October 21–24. That four-day block is set aside specifically for international visitors and is booked directly through Creatrip, a travel platform built for non-Korean travelers — no lottery, no Korean phone number needed. If your trip lands outside that window, the realistic fallback is watching TicketLink for cancellations, or simply doing the cheaper general night viewing instead (see below).

That foreigner-only window closed on October 24, 2026, along with the rest of the season. The details above are kept for reference and as a guide to how next year's program will likely run — check back closer to next autumn for the 2027 dates.

What to Pair It With Nearby

Since each session is about two hours and starts in the early evening, most visitors have time to combine it with another stop either before or after. Everything below is walkable from Gyeongbokgung Station.

Bukchon Hanok Village — A hillside neighborhood of traditional hanok houses a short walk northeast of the palace. Go in the late afternoon for good light, then head to Gyeongbokgung as it gets dark.

Seochon Village — The quieter, more local alternative to Bukchon, just west of the palace. Fewer tour groups, more small cafes and galleries.

National Palace Museum of Korea — Right next to the palace near Exit 5. If you arrive early for a timed session, this is the obvious way to spend the wait instead of standing around outside.

Insadong — A short ride or walk from Gyeongbokgung, with traditional tea houses and restaurants — the easiest nearby spot for dinner before your session.

Where to Stay Nearby

If you want to walk to Gyeongbokgung rather than commute in for your session, the area around Gwanghwamun, Jongno, and Seochon has the highest concentration of options within a 10–15 minute walk of the palace:

  • Nine Tree Premier Hotel Insadong — a short walk from both Insadong and the palace, popular for its breakfast and central location.
  • The Prime Hotel Jongno — close to Jonggak and Anguk stations, a budget-friendlier option still within easy reach of Gyeongbokgung.
  • Hanok guesthouses in Seochon and Bukchon — independently run traditional hanok stays, often booked through Airbnb, if you want a quieter, more local-feeling base rather than a standard hotel.

Availability and pricing shift constantly in this area, especially around festival dates, so check a booking platform for current rates rather than relying on a fixed price here.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the night program cost?

60,000 KRW per person, which includes the guided route and a traditional food tasting. This is separate from general night viewing (around 3,000 KRW), which has no guide or food included.

I don't speak Korean — can I still book?

Yes, if your trip falls on October 21–24 — that window is reserved for foreign visitors and booked directly through Creatrip, no lottery or Korean phone number required. Outside those dates, the general-admission lottery on TicketLink has already closed for this run, so your options are more limited.

My trip doesn't land on October 21–24 — am I out of luck?

For the ticketed special program, mostly yes, since the general lottery closed in mid-August. Two fallbacks: watch TicketLink for cancellations closer to your date, or do the cheaper general night viewing (around 3,000 KRW, no guide or food, but still gets you the lit-up palace at night) instead.

What time do sessions start and end?

Two sessions per operating night: 6:20–8:10 PM and 7:30–9:20 PM, each about 110 minutes.

Is it running every night through October 24?

No — Wednesday through Sunday only, and not at all between September 30 and October 4.

What subway line and exit should I use?

Line 3, Gyeongbokgung Station, Exit 5, which leads directly to the palace grounds.

What else is within walking distance?

Bukchon Hanok Village, Seochon Village, and the National Palace Museum of Korea are all a few minutes' walk from the palace entrance, and several hotels and hanok guesthouses sit within a 10–15 minute walk.
