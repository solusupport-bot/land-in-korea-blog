Most Tourists Lose Their Korea Tax Refund Before They Even Reach the Airport

Everyone tells you to "ask for the Tax Free form" when shopping in Korea. That's the easy part. The part that actually loses people their refund happens later, and it's not about paperwork at all.

The mistake: packing the item before you refund it

Tax Free items have to be shown, unused, at the refund step — either an in-store instant refund or an airport kiosk before you check your bag. If you've already packed the item into checked luggage by the time you reach the refund kiosk, you can't show it, and the refund gets denied on the spot. This happens constantly with clothing and shoes bought early in a trip and packed away days before departure.

The fix: refund before you check in, not after

Keep anything you're claiming Tax Free on in your carry-on (or hold it separately) until you've actually processed the refund at the airport kiosk. If a store offered an instant refund at checkout, you're already done — that's the least error-prone option, and worth asking about before you buy at bigger stores near Myeongdong or Hongdae.

The sticker check most people skip

Not every store with Korean-tourist-friendly pricing actually participates in Tax Free. Look for the Tax Free / Tax Refund sticker at the register before you buy — asking after the fact doesn't help if the store isn't affiliated. Keep your passport on you while shopping; it's required to issue the form at checkout.

One more thing: the minimum spend is per receipt, not per trip

The refund threshold applies per single receipt at a participating store, not your total spending across a trip. Consolidating purchases at one store instead of spreading them across several small ones is often the difference between qualifying and not.
