Jeju Island - What First-Timers Actually Need to Know

No trains, no KTX

Mainland Korea's rail network is one of its biggest conveniences for travelers, which is exactly why Jeju surprises so many first-timers: the island isn't reachable by train at all. Getting there means a flight or a long ferry from the mainland — there's no KTX extension across the water.

What makes it different from the mainland

Jeju is a volcanic island centered on Hallasan, South Korea's tallest mountain, climbable as a long day trip for visitors with the time and fitness for it. The island also has black sand beaches and a local dialect distinct enough that some words differ noticeably from standard Korean.

Rent a car — seriously

Public transit exists on Jeju, but bus routes are noticeably sparser than in Seoul or Busan, and the real distances between sights tend to be longer than they look on a map. A rental car matters more here than almost anywhere else in Korea; it's less a convenience than the practical way most visitors actually get around.

Pack for changeable coastal weather

Weather on Jeju's coast shifts quickly, even in summer. A packable rain layer is worth including regardless of season — the forecast in the morning doesn't always hold by afternoon.

The short version

  • No train or KTX access — only flight or ferry
  • Home to Hallasan, Korea's tallest mountain, plus black sand beaches
  • Renting a car is the practical way to get around
  • Coastal weather changes fast — pack a rain layer even in summer
